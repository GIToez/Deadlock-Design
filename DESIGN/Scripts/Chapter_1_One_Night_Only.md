## SCENE 1 — HIGHWAY 13 / DRIVE INTO RAVENWOOD

**NIGHT — SEVERE STORM**

Black screen. The storm arrives before anything else does. Rain hammering asphalt. Windshield
wipers working hard and barely keeping up. The low roll of thunder somewhere ahead in the dark.
Tires pushing through standing water on a road that keeps threatening to disappear entirely.

Fade in. Jim Mercer's Jeep moves through the storm along Highway 13. The headlights don't reach
far. Road signs appear and vanish in the rain: **BLACKWATER. COUNTY LINE. RAVENWOOD — 2 MILES.**

A radio station cuts weakly through static — barely holding its signal against the weather.

> **RADIO HOST:** *"State police are advising motorists to avoid county roads north of Blackwater
> due to severe flooding. Conditions are expected to worsen through—"*

Static swallows the rest. Jim reaches forward and clicks the radio off. The storm fills the
silence immediately.

Lightning splits the sky somewhere over the tree line to the east. **For a fraction of a second —
something stands near the trees at the edge of the road.** Gone before the eye can find it
properly. Jim doesn't notice. His eyes are on the road. The Jeep pushes on through the dark.

*Gameplay note: the player has light driving control during this scene.*

The phone connects through the Jeep speakers. Caller ID: **SARAH.**

> **SARAH:** *"Please tell me you're parked somewhere."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Technically I'm moving."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Jim."*
>
> **JIM:** *"GPS says another hour. Hour and a half maybe."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Your GPS took you through a field in Ohio."*
>
> **JIM:** *"The road was technically—"*
>
> **SARAH:** *"It was a field."*

Thunder rolls overhead. Jim adjusts his grip on the wheel.

> **JIM:** *"Visibility's bad. I'll stop if it gets worse."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"It already sounds worse."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Three days offline, Sarah. Company's been on me since Monday."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"The relay can wait one more night."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Twelve counties lost coverage."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Twelve counties survived before cell phones."*

He almost laughs. Almost. Lightning flashes again across the tree line. Something near the road.
Gone.

> **JIM:** *"There's a town coming up. I'll grab a room if it looks decent."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"If it looks decent. That's your bar."*
>
> **JIM:** *"It's almost midnight. Bar's whatever's open."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Text me when you stop."*
>
> **JIM:** *"I will."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"I mean it."*
>
> **JIM:** *"I know you mean it."*

Static briefly swallows the line.

> **SARAH:** *"—Jim?"*
>
> **JIM:** *"Still here. Go to sleep."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Not until you text me."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Goodnight Sarah."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Goodnight."*

The call ends. Rain hammers the windshield. The wipers keep their rhythm. The dark road ahead
offers nothing back. Then — through the trees — the faint orange glow of a neon sign bleeds
through the storm. The Jeep slows. A highway exit sign catches the headlights for just a moment:
**RAVENWOOD EXIT.** Jim takes it.

*Full driving control ends. Transition to Scene 2.*

## SCENE 2 — RAVENWOOD HOTEL EXTERIOR / PARKING LOT

**NIGHT — SEVERE STORM**

The Jeep turns into the Ravenwood Hotel parking lot and the headlights sweep across wet asphalt, a
row of parked cars, and the hotel itself — a two-story brick building that looked like it was built
to last and has been quietly losing that argument ever since. The neon sign above the entrance
buzzes in the rain:

> **RAVENWOOD HOTEL**
> **EST. 1956**
> **VACANCY**

One letter in VACANCY flickers. The gutters along the roofline are overwhelmed — sheets of water
pour off the edges and hit the pavement in curtains.

Jim pulls into a spot near the entrance and cuts the engine. Without the Jeep running the storm
becomes the whole world. Rain on the roof. Rain on the asphalt. Thunder somewhere behind the tree
line to the north. He sits there for a moment.

Near the hotel entrance a police cruiser sits with its lights running — red and blue washing
across the wet pavement in slow pulses. An officer (**Dale Pruitt**) stands beside a stopped
vehicle just beyond the lot entrance. One hand rests near his belt. His posture is very still for
someone standing in a downpour. The stopped driver is hard to make out through the rain and the
strobing light. Head down. Shoulders slightly wrong somehow — a subtle hitch in the way they're
sitting that doesn't quite read as normal. Nothing Jim can put a name to. Just something that
doesn't sit right for a half second before the rain shifts and the moment passes.

Jim gets out of the Jeep.

*Player control begins. The player may explore the parking lot before entering the hotel.*

### Optional interaction — open trunk

Jim pops the Jeep's rear hatch. Inside — a duffel bag shoved against one side. A hard case for
survey equipment. A toolbox with a broken latch held shut with a bungee cord. Road flares. A rain
jacket balled up in the corner that he keeps meaning to hang up properly. He looks at it for a
moment.

> **JIM:** *"Not unpacking in this."*

He closes the hatch.

### Optional interaction — examine hotel sign

Jim looks up at the buzzing neon.

> **JIM:** *"One night. That's all I need."*

### Optional interaction — examine storm drain

Water rushes hard into the drain near the parking lot curb — a small torrent fighting to keep up
with the rain. Somewhere below the grate something rattles metallically. Then goes quiet.

> **JIM:** *"This whole lot's going to flood by morning."*

### Optional interaction — approach police stop

Jim moves a few steps toward the cruiser. The officer notices immediately. He doesn't turn fully —
just shifts his weight and cuts a look in Jim's direction without taking his eyes completely off
the stopped vehicle.

> **OFFICER (PRUITT):** *"Hotel's open. Head inside."*

Jim glances toward the stopped car. The driver's head is still down. That shoulder twitch again —
small, rhythmic, like something working just beneath the surface.

> **JIM:** *"Everything alright?"*
>
> **OFFICER (PRUITT):** *"Get inside sir."*

The stopped driver jerks suddenly — a sharp full-body movement that rocks the vehicle slightly.
The officer turns back immediately.

> **OFFICER (PRUITT):** *"Hey. Hands where I can see them. Right now."*

Jim takes the hint. He turns and walks toward the hotel entrance. Behind him the officer's voice
continues — low and controlled — the tone of someone who has already decided this situation is
about to get significantly worse.

*The player approaches the hotel entrance. Jim's phone buzzes.*

## SCENE 3 — SARAH FOLLOW-UP CALL

Jim stops under the narrow overhang above the hotel entrance. It barely qualifies as shelter —
rain blows sideways off the edge and soaks the left side of him anyway. He answers.

> **SARAH:** *"Please tell me that's you stopping somewhere."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Ravenwood Hotel. Est. 1956."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"That's either charming or a tetanus risk."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Probably both."*

He glances back toward the cruiser. The officer still hasn't moved far from the stopped vehicle.
The red and blue light keeps its slow pulse across the wet lot.

> **SARAH:** *"You sound tired."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Been driving since four."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"You told me six this morning."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Did I?"*
>
> **SARAH:** *"Jim."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Few hours sleep and I'll finish the drive in the morning. I'll make the site by
> noon."*

A pause. Rain fills the space between them.

> **SARAH:** *"Okay."*

Just that. The way she says it when she's decided not to push.

> **JIM:** *"I'll text you when I'm checked in."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"You'd better."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Hey."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"What."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Go to sleep."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"I was asleep. You woke me up."*
>
> **JIM:** *"You called me."*
>
> **SARAH:** *"...Goodnight Jim."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Night."*

The call ends. Jim stands under the overhang a moment longer. The police lights pulse across the
lot. The rain keeps coming. He turns toward the hotel doors.

## SCENE 4 — HOTEL ENTRY

The entrance doors are heavy glass — not automatic. Jim pulls one open against the resistance of
the wind and steps through. The door swings shut behind him. The storm disappears. Not entirely —
it's still out there, still audible — but suddenly it's on the other side of something solid and
the contrast is immediate and physical. Warm air. Old carpet. The faint smell of a building that
has been the same building for a very long time. Somewhere overhead a jazz record plays through
speakers that have seen better decades. Jim stands in the entrance for a moment and lets his eyes
adjust.

*Transition to Scene 5.*

## SCENE 5 — HOTEL LOBBY / INITIAL ENTRY

**NIGHT — STORM OUTSIDE**

The Ravenwood Hotel lobby is the kind of room that was probably considered elegant in 1956 and has
been coasting on that reputation ever since. The carpet is deep red and worn thin along the
traffic lines. The wallpaper has a pattern that might have been fashionable once. The furniture is
heavy and mismatched in the way of things that have been replaced one piece at a time over several
decades without anyone ever stepping back to look at the whole picture.

But it's warm. The lighting is low and amber. The jazz coming through the overhead speakers is
quiet enough to be ignored and present enough to fill the silence. Outside the storm pushes
against the windows and doesn't get in.

Jim takes it in from the entrance.

To his left — the west lounge. A large red couch faces a coffee table stacked with old magazines
and hotel brochures. A standing lamp throws a circle of warm light across the cushions. A woman
(**Cindy Sweets**) sits there with her legs crossed, one arm draped over the back of the couch,
watching the rain move across the windows with the particular stillness of someone who has made
peace with being exactly where they are for the foreseeable future.

To his right — a smaller waiting area. Individual chairs along the wall. A side table with a dim
lamp. A vending machine humming quietly in the corner. A woman (**Maria Dalton**) sits in one of
the chairs with one hand resting near her stomach, her posture the careful stillness of someone
who has found the one position that works and is committed to it. A man (**Richard Dalton**)
stands nearby — checking the vending machine selections with the focused attention of someone who
needs something to do with his hands.

Deeper in — near a hallway lamp — a third woman (**Janeth Caldwell**) sits alone with a book open
across her knees. She doesn't look up when Jim enters.

Straight ahead — the reception desk. A brass lamp. A registry book. A man (**Earl Whitaker**)
behind the counter who looks like he has been awake for longer than is strictly advisable and is
handling it with professional dignity.

A television mounted near the desk plays a weather broadcast at low volume. The signal drifts
occasionally into static.

> **TV ANNOUNCER** *(background):* *"—multiple accidents reported along Highway 13 due to severe
> weather conditions — state police advising—"*

Static takes it briefly. The broadcast returns.

Jim crosses the lobby toward the reception desk.

*Player control returns. The player may explore the lobby before approaching the desk.*

---

## SCENE 6 — HOTEL CHECK-IN

The man behind the reception desk looks up from the registry book as Jim approaches. His name tag
reads EARL. He has the kind of face that has settled into itself completely — not unkind, just
finished becoming what it was going to be a long time ago.

> **EARL:** *"Rough night to be drivin'."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Wasn't exactly planning on stopping here."*

Earl pulls the registry book toward him without any particular urgency.

> **EARL:** *"Storm's been knocking power out all evening. You picked a hell of a night to pass
> through Ravenwood."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Seems like."*

Thunder cracks overhead — close enough that it comes through the walls rather than just the
windows. The lobby lights dip for a moment. Everyone in the room reacts in their own small way —
Cindy glances up from the couch, Maria shifts in her chair, Richard looks at the ceiling, Janeth
lowers her book slightly. The lights hold. The television static clears. Nobody says anything.

Earl runs his finger down the registry page.

> **EARL:** *"Need a room?"*
>
> **JIM:** *"Depends. You got anything dry?"*

Earl allows himself a tired half-smile.

> **EARL:** *"Bare minimum requirement around here."*

While Earl searches the registry — Cindy speaks without looking away from the rain-covered
windows.

> **CINDY:** *"Road bad out there?"*
>
> **JIM:** *"Wouldn't recommend it."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Yeah. No shit."*

She goes back to watching the rain. Across the lobby, Richard crouches slightly beside Maria's
chair — quiet enough that the words don't carry, but the posture says everything. She says
something back and he nods. Ordinary. Completely ordinary.

Earl stops at a page.

> **EARL:** *"Room 104. End of the west hall. Stairs are right past the lounge."*

He slides a brass room key across the counter. The tag reads **104.** Jim picks it up.

> **JIM:** *"Appreciate it."*
>
> **EARL:** *"Thirty-two years. I know where everything is."*

From somewhere above them — a distant thud. Heavy enough that the brass lamp on the desk shifts
slightly. Jim glances toward the ceiling. Across the lobby Cindy looks up from the couch. Maria
shifts in her chair. Nobody says anything. Earl doesn't even look up from the registry.

> **EARL:** *"Storm gets into the upper floors sometimes. Guests leave windows cracked, pressure
> does the rest."*

He closes the registry book.

> **EARL:** *"Old building makes all kinds of noise."*

He says it the way a man says something he genuinely believes — not performing calm, just worn
down to it. Jim looks at the ceiling a moment longer. Nothing follows.

> **JIM:** *"Right. Night."*
>
> **EARL:** *"Night."*

Earl is already reaching for the next piece of paperwork.

*Player control returns.*

> *Design note: Earl is written here with zero foreknowledge and zero suspicious behavior — see
> [`CANON.md`](../CANON.md) → "Retcons." The thud should unsettle the player because nobody investigates it
> strongly enough, not because Earl acts like he's hiding something.*

## SCENE 9 — MAIN BAR

The bar is open in the technical sense — the lights are on and nobody has locked the door. In
every other sense it has clearly called it a night. Most of the overhead fixtures are off. A few
amber pendant lights hang above the counter throwing small circles of warm light across the bartop
and not much else.

Glasses hang upside down in their rack above the bar. Liquor bottles line the shelves behind the
counter — the good ones in front, the dusty ones pushed to the back. Barstools sit at irregular
intervals, a couple of them slightly turned as if their last occupants just stepped away. The bar
is empty. No staff. No customers. A radio behind the counter crackles softly — intermittent static
eating at whatever station it was tuned to. A hallway beyond the far end of the bar runs deeper
into the west wing and disappears into the dark.

*The player may inspect: liquor shelves, bar counter, an old jukebox near the wall, rain-covered
windows, stacked glasses.*

### Optional interaction — jukebox

Jim looks at the jukebox. The display is dark. Unplugged.

> **JIM:** *"Probably for the best."*

### Optional interaction — liquor shelves

Jim scans the bottles.

> **JIM:** *"Decent selection for a place like this."*

He doesn't take anything.

## SCENE 10 — RECREATION ROOM

The recreation room sits just off the west lounge. Pool table. Dart board. A card table with
mismatched chairs pushed in at angles. A vending machine in the corner next to an old television on
a wheeled stand. The room is empty.

During a roll of thunder the building trembles slightly and one of the pool balls rolls a few
inches across the felt. Stops. Jim watches it. It doesn't move again.

### Optional interaction — television

Jim clicks the television on. The picture comes up through static — a news broadcast trying to
hold its signal.

> **NEWS REPORTER:** *"—authorities continue to advise residents to remain indoors as flooding
> spreads across the county—"*

Static takes it. Comes back.

> **NEWS REPORTER:** *"—several roads remain impassable and emergency services have reported—"*

Static. Gone completely. Jim clicks it off.

At the far end of the west side a doorway sits in a short alcove. A soft red glow bleeds beneath

## SCENE 12 — GERTA

Jim is moving back through the west hallway when he hears it — the rhythmic squeak of wheels on
carpet, slow and unhurried, coming from somewhere ahead. A heavy-set woman (**Gerta**) in a
housekeeping uniform rounds the corner pushing a cleaning cart. The cart is loaded — towels,
cleaning supplies, a bucket hanging off one side. A flickering ceiling light catches her face for
a moment as she passes. She looks at Jim the way hotel staff look at guests at midnight — not
unfriendly, just focused on something else.

> **GERTA:** *"Evenin'."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Hey."*

She pushes the cart past him and disappears around the far corner. The squeak of the wheels fades.
The hallway settles back into quiet.

## SCENE 13 — STAIRWELL TO ROOM 104

The stairwell is noticeably quieter than the lobby. The carpet continues up the stairs but the
wallpaper changes — older up here, a slightly different pattern, the kind of detail that suggests
the lobby got renovated sometime in the last twenty years and the upper floors didn't. The
lighting is dimmer. The thunder sounds slightly more present without the lobby noise to compete
with it.

Jim climbs. The second floor hallway stretches in both directions at the top of the stairs. Guest
room doors line both sides — all closed, all dark beneath the frames. The overhead lights buzz
faintly. A section near the far end flickers once and holds. Somewhere down the hall a television
murmurs through a thin wall — too quiet to make out. Two rooms down, a brief eruption of muffled
arguing — a man's voice, a woman's voice, sharp and short. Then silence. Whatever it was, it
resolved itself or stopped trying.

Jim finds Room 104 at the end of the west corridor. Room 106 sits a few doors back the way he
came.

*The player may optionally detour to Room 106 before entering Room 104 — see Scene 14. Otherwise,
Jim fits the key to 104. The lock turns.*

## SCENE 14 — CINDY'S DOOR (OPTIONAL)

*Optional. Reachable once Jim is on the second floor, any time before he goes to bed — not tied to
a specific path through the hallway.*

Room 106. Jim knocks.

A beat. Footsteps. The door opens on the chain first — Cindy checking who it is — then swings the
rest of the way.

She's changed out of what she was wearing in the lobby. Robe, hotel-issue, cinched loosely enough
that it's clearly not for anyone's benefit but her own comfort. Hair down. She leans in the
doorway like she's been expecting someone more interesting than housekeeping and has decided to
be gracious about the disappointment anyway.

> **CINDY:** *"Well. Look who found the stairs."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Wanted to make sure Room 104 hasn't collapsed into the parking lot yet."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Very heroic of you."*

She looks him over, unhurried, the kind of look that isn't subtle about being a look.

> **CINDY:** *"You clean up alright for a guy who's been driving through a hurricane."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Low bar tonight."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"I like a low bar."*

A beat. She tips her head slightly toward the room behind her — not quite an invitation, close
enough to one that Jim clocks it.

> **CINDY:** *"Storm's not letting up. Room's got a second chair, if the west lounge got old."*

Jim holds up the room key between two fingers, easy, no offense taken and none intended.

> **JIM:** *"Tempting. I've got a wife who'd know exactly what I was thinking about ten seconds
> before I thought it."*

Cindy laughs — a real one, not offended, more like she respects the answer.

> **CINDY:** *"Smart and taken. Rare combination."*
>
> **JIM:** *"I get that a lot."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Liar."*

She leans against the door frame, arms crossed now, the flirtation settling into something easier
— still warm, just not going anywhere, and both of them fine with that.

> **CINDY:** *"For what it's worth — you're better company than the vending machine."*
>
> **JIM:** *"High praise."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Don't let it go to your head."*

The hallway light overhead buzzes and dips — the same flicker the whole floor's been doing all
night. Cindy glances up at it, and something in her expression shifts, the flirtation dropping
away for a second underneath a genuine, smaller unease.

> **CINDY:** *"...You hear all that noise downstairs earlier?"*
>
> **JIM:** *"Probably pipes."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"You hear that scratching?"*

Jim pauses.

> **JIM:** *"Old building."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Didn't sound old."*

A beat. Then she shakes it off, half a smile coming back, more for her own benefit than his.

> **CINDY:** *"Seriously though — get some sleep. You look like hell."*
>
> **JIM:** *"Working on it."*
>
> **CINDY:** *"Night, Jim."*

She steps back inside. The door doesn't fully shut — just eases most of the way closed, robe-and-
storm-light warm behind it for a second before the latch finally clicks.

> *Design/story note: keep this grounded rather than gratuitous — Cindy is genuinely flirting,
> Jim genuinely (and easily, without moralizing about it) doesn't take her up on it because he's
> established as devoted to Sarah, not because the scene is shying away from adult subject matter.
> This is the version of the "Cindy at her door" beat that plays if the player seeks her out
> proactively. If the player instead simply walks past Room 106 on their way to 104 without
> knocking, nothing plays here — see [`Locations/Ravenwood_Hotel.md`](../Locations/Ravenwood_Hotel.md)
> for how this optional scene fits into the room list.*

## SCENE 15 — ROOM 104 ENTRY

**NIGHT — STORM OUTSIDE**

The room is small, clean, and smells faintly of the same cleaning product used in every hotel
built before 1980. A double bed with a dark green cover. A nightstand with a rotary phone and a
lamp. A dresser with a mirror above it. A luggage rack near the door that Jim ignores. A standing
lamp in the corner. A window with heavy curtains facing the parking lot.

Jim sets the room key on the nightstand and closes the door behind him. For the first time since he
left the highway the world is quiet. Just the rain against the glass and the low movement of
thunder working its way east. He stands there for a moment doing nothing in particular.

*Player control returns.*

### Optional interaction — window

Jim pulls the curtain aside an inch. The parking lot below is dark and rain-soaked. The police
cruiser is still there — lights still running, slow red and blue across the wet asphalt. But the
officer is gone. The stopped vehicle sits alone. No one visible inside from this angle. Lightning
flashes. In the half-second of white light — **something moves near the cruiser. Low. Fast.**
Gone before Jim's eyes can find it. He lets the curtain fall.

> **JIM:** *"...Probably just the storm."*

### Optional interaction — bed

Jim looks at the bed with the focused appreciation of a man who has been in a car for eight hours.

> **JIM:** *"Looks better than the Jeep."*

### Optional interaction — television

The television crackles to life.

> **NEWS REPORTER:** *"State authorities continue advising residents to avoid travel due to severe
> flooding and multiple roadway accidents throughout the county—"*

The signal distorts. Rolls. Comes back. Then — cutting through the static completely — a burst of
radio dispatch.

## SCENE 17 — THE WAKE-UP

**NIGHT — OUTBREAK HAS BEGUN**

Black screen. Rain. Distant thunder. Then — **a civil emergency siren tears through the night**
somewhere outside the hotel. Deep and mechanical — the kind of sound designed to reach people
through walls and sleep and denial. Jim's eyes open immediately. Red emergency light pulses through
the curtains in slow rhythmic flashes. The siren continues — rising and falling, relentless.

hitting the floor, glass breaking. A woman crying somewhere in the building. Running footsteps in

> **JIM:** *"What the fuck—"*

## SCENE 21 — LOBBY REVEAL

The lobby at the bottom of the stairs is not the lobby Jim checked into four hours ago. The
furniture is overturned. Glass covers the floor near the entrance. The front doors are partially
open and rain blows through in sheets, soaking the carpet and the broken things lying across it.
The television near reception shows almost pure static — a high thin whine beneath the noise. The
jazz is gone. Red emergency light from outside pulses across everything. Slow. Steady. Making the
shadows move.

Near the reception desk — near where Earl was standing with his paperwork — something crouches over
a shape on the floor. Jim's eyes find it and take a moment to understand what they're seeing. The
shape on the floor is a person. A hotel guest — one of the ones Jim doesn't recognize from the
lobby earlier. They're still moving. Weakly. One hand opening and closing against the carpet. The
sound coming from the thing above them is wet and rhythmic — not chewing, exactly, more like
something tearing loose and being worked free.

The thing crouching over them lifts its head. **It's Earl.** Blood covers his mouth, his chin, the
front of his vest — a dark, glistening mess that's soaked through the fabric. There's a wound at
the guest's throat that Jim's brain refuses to fully render for a second, the visual arriving in
pieces rather than all at once. Earl's eyes are wrong — pale and flat and lit with something that
has nothing to do with recognition. His jaw hangs at an angle that jaws don't hang at, unhinged
and loose like the muscle holding it in place gave up hours ago.

He looks at Jim. Jim doesn't move.

> **JIM:** *"Earl — Jesus, what the fuck—"*

The rest of the sentence doesn't come. There isn't one. Earl rises from the body. Slow. Stiff.
Like a man learning to stand again.

## SCENE 22 — FIRST COMBAT / BASEBALL BAT

Jim backs away. His heel catches a piece of overturned furniture and his hand goes down to catch

small adjustments a walking person makes without thinking about it. His mouth opens slightly. A
sound comes out of it that Jim will not be able to describe afterward.

> **JIM:** *"...You've gotta be kidding me."*

*First combat begins.*

The bat connects on the first swing with a sound Jim's never heard a human body make — a wet,
solid crack that should drop a man and doesn't. Earl staggers, rights himself, keeps coming. It
takes three more full swings before he goes down and stays down — the last one caving in something
that shouldn't cave that easily. Jim's hands are shaking on the grip. He doesn't let go of it.

*Mid-combat — from the top of the staircase:*

> **JANETH:** *"Oh my God—!"*

Jim looks up. Janeth stands at the top of the stairs gripping the railing — book still in her hand,

and worse.

> **JIM:** *"Get downstairs — go for the door—!"*
>
> **JANETH:** *"What is wrong with him—?!"*
>
> **JIM:** *"GO—!"*

## SCENE 23 — POLICE CRUISER CRASH / JANETH'S DEATH

Headlights explode through the lobby windows. No warning. No sound of brakes. Just the white-hot

hotel.** The doors detonate inward. Glass turns to a horizontal wall of fragments. Furniture
launches sideways. The cruiser's front end plows through the lobby entrance and doesn't stop until
it hits the support pillar near the center of the room. The sound is enormous and then it's over
and there is just the hiss of the engine and the rain pouring through the destroyed entrance and
the slow creaking of something structural deciding whether or not to hold.

Janeth had been three steps from the door. She is pinned between the pillar and the cruiser's
crumpled hood — bent at an angle a body isn't built to bend at, blood already spreading dark and
fast across the front of her coat and pooling on the marble beneath her. Her book lies on the wet
carpet four feet away, soaking through, the pages going dark. She doesn't move. The front exit is
gone. The emergency siren outside cuts off. Silence — real silence — drops over Ravenwood for just
a moment.

> **JIM:** *"...No. No, no — "*

He doesn't finish it either. There's nothing to finish it with.

Then the police radio in the wrecked cruiser crackles.

> **POLICE RADIO:** *"—all remaining units — Deadlock Protocol is now in effect. Repeat — Deadlock

Static. Outside — distant gunfire. The quarantine loudspeaker somewhere across town.

> **LOUDSPEAKER:** *"ALL CIVILIANS ARE ORDERED TO REMAIN INDOORS—"*

Static. The hotel lights die completely. Emergency backup lighting activates throughout the lobby —
red, dim, pulsing. Jim stands in what used to be the hotel entrance with a baseball bat and blood on
his hands that isn't his.

**TITLE CARD: DEADLOCK PROTOCOL**

## SCENE 37 — THE LAUNDRY ROOM

*Optional. Reached by following the blood trail through the staff storage door behind the hotel
bar (Scene 36).*

The staff storage door opens onto a short, low-ceilinged utility corridor that Jim didn't know
existed a few hours ago — the kind of space guests never see and staff barely think about.
Warmer back here than the rest of the hotel; the air is thick with the smell of detergent and hot,
stale steam that hasn't fully cleared. The corridor ends at the hotel laundry room.

Industrial washers and dryers line one wall, a couple of them still running — one thumping
rhythmically through an unbalanced load nobody's coming back to fix. Rolling laundry carts sit
half-loaded with towels and sheets. A rack of staff uniforms hangs along the far wall, undisturbed.

The blood trail doesn't stop at the doorway. It continues across the floor, between two of the
carts, and ends at a housekeeping uniform crumpled against the base of a dryer.

It's **Gerta.**

She's on her back, one arm still tangled in the strap of the bucket she was carrying earlier that
same night. Whatever came for her didn't leave much time for her to understand what was happening —
there's no barricade attempted, no sign of a struggle that went anywhere. Just the one location,
and the aftermath. Her uniform is torn open across the torso in a way that isn't a tear so much as
an absence — a section of her simply isn't there anymore, the edges of it wrong in a way that
doesn't read as claws or teeth.

The wound pattern doesn't match Earl. Doesn't match anything Jim's fought so far. Something about
it is wrong in a different, more specific way — less like teeth and hands, more like something
closed around her all at once.

> **JIM:** *"Oh — Jesus. Jesus Christ."*

Jim crouches beside her for a moment. Doesn't touch her.

> **JIM:** *"...Hey. Hey, it's alright."*

It isn't, and he knows it isn't, and he says it anyway, the way people say things to the dead that
they'd say to the living, because the alternative is saying nothing at all.

A scrape of something dragging against metal — low, deliberate — comes from beyond the far wall,
past a gap where a service panel has been pried loose. For half a second, low to the ground and
folding itself through the gap, something moves that is not quite the right shape for a person —
long arms, a hunched and narrow torso, gone before Jim's eyes can hold onto it. Not toward Jim.
Away from him. Whatever did this isn't finished being somewhere else tonight.

> *Design/story note: this is the same presence that dragged Cindy Sweets back into Room 106 (see
> Scene 35) — barely glimpsed there as "something," "fast — too fast to properly see." It's since
> been fully designed as **The Maw** (see [`Creatures/The_Maw.md`](../Creatures/The_Maw.md)) — a heavily mutated Ashen Mutant
> whose torso splits open into a feeding mouth, ordinarily kept hidden by its hunched, "closed"
> posture. This glimpse is intentionally brief and unclear, consistent with its design: seeing it
> does not mean fighting it. This is currently the only confirmed kill attributed to it; Cindy's
> own fate remains a separate, still-open question — see [`STORY_NOTES.md`](../STORY_NOTES.md).*

## SCENE 40 — THE RED ROOM (OPTIONAL)

### Optional interaction — booking slip

## SCENE 41 — INFECTED OFFICER / HANDGUN

Jim crosses the lobby again. The cruiser. The rain. The destroyed entrance. He moves past the hood
of the cruiser — and the passenger door explodes outward. No warning. No sound from inside. Just
the door hitting its hinges hard and a shape coming through it fast — a police officer, uniform
dark with blood, one eye clouded completely white, moving with the same locked-in wrongness as
Earl but faster, more recent, less settled into it.

> **JIM:** *"Son of a—"*

He swings the bat.

*Combat.* It's faster and meaner than Earl was, and it costs Jim a torn sleeve and a graze along
the forearm before it goes down for good, blood — his and the officer's both — spattered across
the wrecked reception counter.

After. Jim crouches beside the officer. He looks at the face for a moment. The parking lot. The
stopped vehicle. The officer who told him to get inside.

> **JIM:** *"...You had no idea what you were pulling over."*

He searches the duty belt.

*ITEM ACQUIRED: HANDGUN*

## SCENE 42 — HOTEL COURTYARD

The exterior security gate opens into the storm. Jim steps outside for the first time since he
parked the Jeep. The rain hits him immediately — cold and heavy, the kind that soaks through in
thirty seconds regardless of what you're wearing. The courtyard stretches out ahead — enclosed on
all sides by the hotel walls and a perimeter fence. Stone walkways cracked and dark with water. A
fountain at the center, overflowing, water running off across the pavement in every direction.
Overgrown hedges along the fence line. Rusted benches nobody has sat on in years. Emergency
floodlights mounted along the hotel exterior throwing pale uneven light across the wet stone.

At the far end — the north gate. Heavy. Closed. A small warning light beside the gate mechanism
blinks red.

*Interaction prompt: [USE GATE]*

**ACCESS DENIED. GENERATOR FAILURE.**

> **JIM:** *"You've gotta be kidding me..."*

In the rear corner of the courtyard — a fenced maintenance area contains a silent industrial
backup generator. Electrical conduit running from it toward the gate system. Beside the
generator — a maintenance shed. Heavy padlocked doors. Something has warped them badly from
inside. Jim looks at the shed for a moment. Then he goes to work on the generator.

## SCENE 43 — GENERATOR RESTORATION

The generator hasn't been started in a while — that much is obvious. Fuel valve partially closed.
Pressure dropped. Ignition contacts corroded at the terminals. Jim works through it methodically.
Fuel valve open. Terminals cleaned with what he has available. Pressure checked. Ignition primed.
He's done this kind of thing in worse conditions than a rainstorm in a hotel courtyard. Different
equipment, different location, same basic logic — find the problem, address it in the right order,
don't rush the steps.

He hits the ignition. The generator turns over. Coughs. Dies. He adjusts the choke. Tries again.

**The generator roars to life.** The entire courtyard shakes from the startup. Floodlights along
the hotel walls blaze on at full power — the courtyard suddenly too bright after hours of
emergency red. The gate system begins powering back online. For a moment — just a moment — the
only sound is the generator and the rain.

Then — **BANG.**

## SCENE 44 — THE CARETAKER (ROY BULLOCK) — BOSS ENCOUNTER

The maintenance shed doors buckle outward. The chain pulls taut — holds — then snaps. The doors
come open. He fills the doorway before he steps through it. Maintenance overalls, darkened with
water and worse. What the infection has done to him has not finished doing it — the proportions
are wrong, one shoulder built up into something that shouldn't fit through a standard doorframe and
barely does. His right arm hangs heavier than an arm should hang. The skin is waterlogged and pale
in the floodlights. One eye glows faintly — not a color that belongs in an eye. He steps out into
the rain. Looks at Jim. A sound comes from somewhere deep in his chest — low and wet and
deliberate.

*Boss encounter begins.*

### Phase 1 — Stalk

He moves across the flooded courtyard pavement with the patience of something that has decided
where it's going and sees no reason to hurry. Wide. Deliberate. Obstacles — a bench, the
fountain's edge, a section of hedge — he moves around them or through them depending on which is
faster. Jim keeps distance. The handgun is an option. So is the bat. Neither one feels adequate
from where he's standing.

### Phase 2 — Aggression

When he closes the distance he commits to it completely. The overhead slam drives into the stone
hard enough to crater it. The horizontal swing clears a radius that Jim has to already be outside
of before it starts. A shoulder charge that covers ground faster than the rest of his movement
suggests is possible. The recovery windows are real. The attacks have weight — they commit the
full mass of him to the motion and then they have to stop. That's when Jim moves.

A glancing hit from the mutated arm still throws Jim off his feet and into the fountain's edge
hard enough to knock the air out of him.

> **JIM:** *"—Christ! Okay. Okay, come on then."*

He's back up before the next swing.

### Phase 3 — Enraged

At some threshold the patience breaks. The deliberate stalking becomes something more reckless —
charges that don't slow down when they miss, slam combinations without the pause between them,
movement that stops caring about obstacles and goes through them instead. The generator floodlights
flicker. The courtyard dims and blazes in uneven intervals. Lightning overhead. The rain driving
sideways now. Jim doesn't stop moving.

The last exchange is close range — the handgun emptied point-blank into the exposed, distended
mass of his throat and chest until whatever's holding him upright finally gives out. He goes down
hard, the impact splitting stone, and doesn't get back up.

*The Caretaker goes down.*

The courtyard falls quiet except for the generator and the rain. Jim stands in the middle of the
wet stone and the overturned furniture of the fight, breathing hard, blood — some of it his, most
of it not — running off him in the downpour. He looks at what's left of the Caretaker. He doesn't
look long.

## SCENE 45 — NORTH GATE / EXIT INTO RAVENWOOD

The north gate system is fully active now.

*Interaction prompt: [OPEN GATE]*

streets. Standing water across the pavement. A traffic light somewhere down the block swinging on
its cable in the wind, cycling through its colors for nobody. Police lights flashing in the

> **JIM:** *"...What the hell happened here?"*

The rain doesn't answer. He steps through the gate.
