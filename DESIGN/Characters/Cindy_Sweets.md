# Cindy Sweets

## Role

Hotel guest, Room 106. Stranded traveler; briefly a friendly presence for Jim pre-outbreak, later
the game's most prominent unresolved-fate character in Chapter 1.

## Background

Was on her way to Asheville; the storm/flooding stranded her in Ravenwood instead. No other
biography established.

## Appearance

Seen pre-outbreak in the west lounge, relaxed, legs crossed. During her Room 106 jumpscare:
barefoot, still in her robe, blood on her face, neck, and the front of the robe.

## Personality

Relaxed, sardonic, direct — the type to say the flippant thing before the sincere thing. Notices
things and says so without dressing it up (*"Town gives me weird vibes. Can't explain it. Just
does."*). Establishes real, if brief, rapport with Jim across two separate conversations, which is
deliberately there to make her later abduction land harder.

## Relationships

Friendly, slightly flirtatious-but-casual rapport with [Jim](Jim_Mercer.md), built across the
pre-outbreak lobby scene and a second encounter at her door before Jim goes to bed. No other
established relationships.

## Story Arc

Introduced in the lobby (Room 106) → a second, uneasy conversation at her door just before Jim
retires for the night (she's already picking up on "scratching" sounds Jim brushes off) → during
the outbreak, Jim finds her door blood-marked and hears a wet dragging sound from inside but
doesn't see her → later, once West Wing power is restored, she bursts out of Room 106 calling for
help and is violently dragged back inside before Jim can reach her; the door slams. Whatever
grabbed her has since been identified — per explicit direction from the project owner
(2026-08-12) — as **The Maw**, the same presence later confirmed to have killed Gerta in the hotel
Laundry Room (see [`Creatures/The_Maw.md`](../Creatures/The_Maw.md)). **Cindy's own ultimate fate remains explicitly
unresolved**, however — identifying her abductor is not the same as resolving what happened to
her. Per [`AI.json`](../AI.json), this was left open on purpose ("we haven't gotten that far in the story yet"),
not simply unwritten. Do not invent a resolution for her without explicit approval.

## Dialogue Characteristics

Short, wry, unbothered on the surface. *"How?"* (in response to "could be worse") is a
representative beat — she'll puncture a platitude rather than accept it. Her unease, when it shows,
shows through specificity rather than panic (*"You hear that scratching?"*).
