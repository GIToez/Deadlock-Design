## Concept

A "rat king"-style mass of infected bodies, fused together by advanced Ashen mutation into a single
rolling, grasping conglomerate — a handful of individual infected at first, eventually dozens,
tangled into one entity. Classified in the concept dossier as **"ZK" — Zombie Conglomerate, Class:
Apex Mutation**. In-world, per the dossier's own tone, this is also colloquially called **"the
Zombie King"** by anyone unlucky enough to survive seeing it and talk about it afterward — the same
initials as its technical designation, treated here as a deliberate, in-world coincidence worth
keeping rather than a naming conflict to resolve.

Unlike every other creature in the game, the Zombie Conglomerate is explicitly **not a fight**
during Chapter 2 — it's a roaming, unpredictable **world/city boss** in the sense of being a
persistent, unique, oversized hazard the player can encounter repeatedly across the whole map
rather than a scripted arena encounter, structurally closer to a natural disaster than a
traditional enemy. Per the dossier: *"There is no killing it. There is only avoiding it."* Whether
that stays true for the entire game, or whether it becomes killable by some later point (e.g. a
Chapter 3 endgame beat), is an open design question — see "Open Design Gaps," below. Nothing in
Chapter 2 should imply it either way; treat it as unkillable for all of Chapter 2 regardless of how
that question is eventually resolved.

## Origin (revised 2026-08-14 — forms during Outbreak Night, not weeks before)

**The Zombie Conglomerate Jim actually encounters forms and grows during the same night as the
outbreak itself.** It is not a pre-existing giant creature that's been roaming or contained for
weeks before Jim ever reaches Ravenwood — that would contradict locked canon (per
[`CANON.md`](../CANON.md), the entire game takes place over one night, and Ravenwood is ordinary
and outbreak-free right up until Jim arrives). What can plausibly predate the outbreak is much
smaller: **Vanguard may have documented early, small-scale fusion phenomena** — isolated incidents
where two or three exposed subjects began merging under lab conditions or in early containment
failures — as a precursor research curiosity, not a public or citywide threat. The actual
city-roaming mass is something new, actively developing in real time over the course of Jim's one
night in Ravenwood, growing visibly larger and more dangerous the longer the outbreak continues
uncontained. If any of the dossier's original "Recorded Sightings" log (previously dated Day
18–45) is retained as an in-world found document, it should be rewritten to describe this kind of
early precursor research — small experimental aggregations, not the enormous creature Jim
encounters — so it no longer contradicts the one-night premise.

It is explicitly **not born, but built** — it grows by consuming biomass, incorporating the dead
and dying (and, implicitly, the living) into its ever-expanding mass, compressed into hours rather
than weeks. This makes it mechanically and thematically distinct from every other Black Vein
presentation documented so far: Shamblers, the Caretaker, and The Maw are each *one* mutated
individual; the Ashen Hound is a mutated *animal*; the Zombie Conglomerate is what happens when
that same mutation is allowed to keep absorbing bodies indefinitely, with no known upper limit —
and, now, visibly happening *while the player watches*, across the course of Chapter 2, rather than
having already happened off-screen before the game begins.

## Appearance

A writhing mass of tangled infected bodies, faces and grasping arms visible across its surface,
growing in size as it incorporates more bodies over the course of the night. Per the dossier, five
referenced growth stages — now understood as happening across Chapter 2's single night rather than
over weeks:

| Stage | Name | Body Count | Approx. Size | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Gathering | 2–3 bodies | ~5 ft | Early fusion; an awkward, unstable knot that flops, drags, or rolls itself along more than it moves with any purpose — easy to mistake for a pile of corpses shifting, or not fully register as a single thing at all |
| 2 | The Emergence | 8–10 bodies | ~10 ft | Dramatically grown from Stage 1; moves with real force now, strong enough to damage or shove aside environmental obstacles — the point where the player realizes it's actively growing between encounters, not just relocating |
| 3 | Consumption | 20+ bodies | ~15–20 ft | Large enough to block pathways/streets outright; announces itself with sound before it's seen |
| 4 | The Wanderer | 40+ bodies | ~25 ft | A city-wide threat, forcing alternate routes through buildings/alleys rather than just around a corner |
| 5 | The Titan | 60+ bodies | ~30–36 ft | "Unstoppable force," effectively owning portions of Downtown |

## Behavior

Per the dossier: aimlessly roams Ravenwood, with no fixed path and no predictable pattern — *"It
appears where it pleases and stays only as long as it feeds. There is no predicting it. Only
preparing for it."* Attracted to noise, heat, and movement. Destroys everything in its path and
absorbs any biomass (dead or alive) it encounters, growing larger over time. Leaves a visible trail
of death and infection behind it. Cannot be killed or meaningfully contained — destroying part of
it has no effect on the whole, since (per the dossier's "Structure" notes) its bodies are tightly
tangled and fused, propelled by multiple limbs acting in loose shared coordination via distributed
nerve clusters, and pain itself is distributed across the mass rather than localized.

## Gameplay Role

Per the project owner's brief: **first appears after Jim collects his first emblem**, and roams the
city for the remainder of Chapter 2's open-world exploration, growing as the night goes on.
Proposed design intent (pending approval):

- **Not a fight, ever, during Chapter 2.** No weapon, including the eventual shotgun, does anything
  to it. The correct player response is always to break line of sight, get indoors, or otherwise
  get out of its path — closer to a stalker/hazard mechanic (in the vein of an unkillable pursuer)
  than any combat encounter in the game so far.
- **Roams unpredictably, not on a fixed route or schedule.** It can plausibly appear on any city
  street between districts once unlocked, forcing the player to stay alert during open-world
  traversal rather than treating the streets as fully safe once cleared of ordinary Shamblers.
- **Growth and presence both scale with crest count, not randomly, and not with real-world/session
  time (locked 2026-08-14).** Since district order is open, tying its growth to "how long since the
  first district" doesn't work for every player — a crest-count gate does, the same convention used
  for Jim's own Chapter 2 arc (see [`Characters/Jim_Mercer.md`](../Characters/Jim_Mercer.md)):
  - **1 crest — first sighting, Stage 1 ("The Gathering").** Only a few infected bodies fused
    together, awkward and unstable — it flops, drags, or rolls itself away rather than moving with
    any real intent. Jim (and the player) may not even fully understand what they just saw; this is
    a "was that...?" moment, not an immediately legible threat.
  - **2 crests — Stage 2 ("The Emergence").** The same thing, dramatically grown: more bodies
    absorbed, moving with much more force, now able to damage or obstruct the environment. This is
    the encounter that makes clear it's actively growing between sightings, not just wandering
    around at a fixed size.
  - **3 crests — Stage 3 ("Consumption").** Heard before seen — the player learns to recognize it
    by sound alone before a sighting confirms it — and large enough to block a specific, familiar
    street outright, forcing a detour.
  - **4 crests — Stage 4 ("The Wanderer").** Forces alternate routes through buildings/interiors
    rather than just around a corner — large enough to change how the player thinks about city
    traversal generally.
  - **5 crests — Stage 5 ("The Titan").** Effectively owns portions of Downtown, visibly present
    rather than randomly encountered. This also gives the city streets themselves a visible,
    escalating arc — and a visual indicator that the outbreak itself keeps getting worse — that
    doesn't depend on which four districts the player actually chose to visit first.
- **Also serves as a natural pacing/tension tool** for the open city sections between districts,
  giving Chapter 2's "safe once cleared" streets an ongoing source of dread the way the Caretaker
  and Fennimore's pincer ambush did for the hotel's back half.
- **Environmental interaction — turning avoidance into a tool.** *"Once it is large enough —
  probably beginning around its second major stage — Jim may deliberately attract it toward
  obstacles such as wrecked vehicles, blocked alleys, weakened walls, debris piles, or other
  obstructions. The creature can smash through those obstacles and create routes Jim could not
  otherwise open."* Consistent with its established "attracted to noise, heat, and movement"
  behavior, its mass can be deliberately baited into rolling through a specific spot — crushing a
  car blocking a shortcut, collapsing debris sealing an alley, etc. — turning what would otherwise
  be an obstacle-clearing key/tool puzzle into "lure the thing you can't fight into doing it for
  you." This is a **dangerous risk/reward interaction, not a free tool**: Jim has to intentionally
  attract something he cannot fight, get it moving toward the correct location, and escape before
  it reaches him. Most plausible starting around Stage 2 (2 crests) — the first stage large and
  forceful enough to actually move heavy obstacles. Not yet designed in full mechanical detail —
  which specific blocked locations use this and exactly how the player baits it to a chosen spot
  (rather than wherever it currently is) are both still open.

## Encounter Progression

Not yet scripted scene-by-scene. First appears once Jim holds his first crest (from whichever
district he visits first), then its presence and growth escalate per the crest-count table above
through the rest of Chapter 2's open-world sections — not on a fixed schedule or tied to a specific
location.

## Major Appearances

None scripted yet.

## Story Significance

Represents the most extreme, unchecked expression of Ashen mutation/Black Vein shown in the game —
per the dossier's own framing, "a failed experiment, a consequence of unchecked exposure." Where
every other infected creature is legible as *a person* (or an animal) who turned, the Zombie
Conglomerate is what happens when that process is allowed to continue absorbing more and more
biomass without intervention — and now, specifically, an argument made physically and in real time
across a single night for why Vanguard's containment collapsing at all is catastrophic on a scale
beyond "some people get infected." Its visible growth over the course of Chapter 2 becomes a
running indicator that the outbreak itself keeps getting worse the longer Jim is out in the city,
independent of anything he personally does — reinforcing the game's broader theme (see
[`CANON.md`](../CANON.md)) that Vanguard's hubris created something its own internal notes admit
they no longer understand or control.

## Open Design Gaps

- No formal combat/avoidance mechanic has been specified (detection range, how the player breaks
  line of sight, what happens if caught, whether "caught" is an instant fail-state or a damage/
  chase sequence).
- Not yet integrated into any [`Scripts/`](../Scripts/) file as an actual scripted or systemic
  encounter — this file establishes concept/lore/behavior only, including the now-locked
  crest-count escalation table above.
- **Whether it's ever killable, deliberately left open (2026-08-14) per the project owner:** *"not
  quite sure if you will be killable by the end — we can figure that one out later."* Chapter 2
  should treat it as strictly unkillable throughout; if a later point in the game (most plausibly a
  Chapter 3 endgame beat, once Jim has left Chapter 2's rules behind) makes it killable, that's a
  decision to make when Chapter 3 is actually designed, not now.
- **The "lure it into clearing an obstacle" interaction (see "Gameplay Role," above) is a concept
  only** — no specific blocked locations, exact bait mechanic, or precise stage/crest gating beyond
  "probably starting around Stage 2" have been chosen yet.
- **If any in-world "early sightings" document is ever written** (see "Origin," above), it should
  describe small-scale precursor fusion research/experimental aggregations — two or three subjects
  at most, Vanguard-internal only — never the city-roaming mass itself, which is new to this night.
